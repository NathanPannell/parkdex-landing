Day trips

Victoria and Oak Bay shoreline

This is the trip for a clear afternoon when you want to be near the water without committing to a big hike. It strings four badge parks along the south and east shore of the city, starting in the middle of Victoria and ending on the sand at Cadboro Bay.

The stops are close together and most of the way follows the waterfront, so it works by car, by bike, or as a long walk with a bus ride home. Start with a full battery; you will want photos.

1Beacon Hill Park

Beacon Hill is the big green heart of downtown, and it rewards wandering. Garden paths loop past ponds, peacocks roam the lawns, and the stone bridge over Goodacre Lake is the spot on the badge, so it makes a good place for your check-in photo.

Before you leave, walk to the southern edge. The Garry oak meadows give way to open views over the Strait of Juan de Fuca, and Mile Zero, the western end of the Trans-Canada Highway, is just nearby.

On to the next one: Head east along the Dallas Road waterfront and pick up Beach Drive into Oak Bay. The water stays on your right for most of the way.

2Willows Park

Willows is the easy, sociable stop. An open beach of driftwood and pebbles sits below wide lawns and mature trees, with a playground for anyone who needs to burn off energy.

The Kiwanis Tea Room is the small building by the beach, and it is a natural place to take a break before the next badge.

On to the next one: Uplands Park is just around the corner from Willows, close enough to walk.

3Uplands Park

Uplands feels wilder than the parks around it. It protects an open Garry oak meadow on shallow, rocky ground, and in spring the meadow fills with camas and other wildflowers.

Follow the paths toward Cattle Point for views back out over the strait. Stay on the trails here; the meadow plants are fragile.

On to the next one: Continue north along the shore to Cadboro Bay to finish.

4Cadboro Gyro Park

The last stop is pure fun. Cadboro-Gyro Park pairs a sandy beach with a marine-themed playground, and its concrete play sculptures are hard to miss: a red octopus, a blue salmon, and Caddy, the green-and-yellow sea serpent.

It is a good place to sit on the sand, check your Dex, and count four new badges.

Still have energy? PKOLS (Mount Douglas Park) is a short drive north and has a badge of its own, with a summit view to end the day.

Check park hours, parking and trail conditions before you go. Each badge unlocks once you’re inside that park’s boundary.
